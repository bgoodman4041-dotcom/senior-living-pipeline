## 0. Executive takeaways (developer lens)

1. **RiverSpring is a land-rich nonprofit health system that happens to own housing, not a housing company.** The cash engine was the Medicaid managed long-term care (MLTC) plan, ElderServe Health (d/b/a RiverSpring Health Plans). It had **$1.27B of 2024 revenue** [V], more than 10 times the nursing home's **$113M** [V]. RiverSpring **ended that affiliation when ElderServe joined CareSource on Sept 1, 2025** [V]. The integrated "health plan + housing + nursing home" story is now partly historical.
2. **The nursing home is losing money and running far below its license.** Hebrew Home had operating deficits of **-$18.1M (2023)** and **-$10.4M (2024)** [V/I, computed from 990 data]. CMS data show about **365 residents in 843 certified beds (43% occupancy)** [V]. Net assets fell from $82.7M (2022) to $59.1M (2024) [V].
3. **The new value-creation play is the land.** RiverSpring is ground-leasing about **3 acres** of its **32-acre** campus (99-year lease) to an affiliate. That affiliate is building **River's Edge**: 260 independent-living units in an Article 46 life-care CCRC, financed with **$632.9M of tax-exempt Build NYC bonds** (closed Feb 2026). The weighted-average entrance fee is **$2.1M** and the monthly fee **$9,200** [V]. Occupancy is targeted for **Dec 2028** [V].
4. **That cost basis is extreme: about $2.43M financed per unit, or about $1,435 per SF** (including capitalized interest and reserves) [I, computed]. It works only because (a) the land was owned and is leased from an affiliate, (b) debt is tax-exempt, (c) about 86% of units were presold to buyers paying 90%-refundable entrance fees of about $2.1M [V], and (d) the existing Hebrew Home bed licence covers the life-care health obligation. **A for-profit developer cannot copy (b) or (d) directly.** It can copy (a) by ground-leasing land from a nonprofit and (c) by partnering with a nonprofit sponsor.
5. **Against Ben's 6.5% minimum yield on cost (YOC):** new-build rental IL/AL/MC in the NY metro core (Westchester, Fairfield, Bergen) is likely to come in at **about 5–6% YOC** [I]. Reaching **6.5%+** needs one or more of these: second-ring land at or below about $300k per acre, an AL/MC-heavy mix, all-in cost at or below about $500–550k per unit, or land contributed or ground-leased by a nonprofit or hospital partner. Also, if stabilized cap rates are about 6.5–7.25% [I], a 6.5% YOC is **at or below exit cap**, which means no development spread. Consider **7.5–8.0% YOC** the practical hurdle for merchant-build value creation.
6. **Regulation decides which components can go where.** New **nursing home beds are effectively unavailable** in all three states. NY uses a need methodology with a presumption of no need when county occupancy is below 97% [V]. NJ cancelled its general long-term-care bed call [V]. CT has had a moratorium for 30+ years [V]. The **main exception is CT, where CCRC-associated beds that do not serve Medicaid patients are exempt** [V]. **Assisted living is the buildable care tier.** It needs no CON in CT [V], an expedited CN in NJ [V], and ALR/EALR licensure in NY. Entrance-fee CCRCs require state certification (NY Article 46 Certificate of Authority, NJ DCA, CT DSS).

**Cost escalation, computed [I]:**
- 2017: $912k per unit before financing (388 units).
- 2019: $1.28M per unit before financing; $838k per unit construction only (about 270 units).
- 2026: **$2.43M per unit total financing** ($632.9M ÷ 260) = **about $1,435 per GSF** including capitalized interest, debt service reserve and costs of issuance.

Between 2019 and 2026, total budget rose about 43% while the unit count fell. NYC high-rise CCRC construction is not a template for suburban product.

**Health-care structure [V]:** River's Edge owns **no** health beds. Hebrew Home provides AL, memory care and SNF by contract, with priority access for residents. The CCRC pays Hebrew Home a projected $0.4M in 2024, rising to $4.7M in 2027 ([2019 disclosure](https://WWW.HEALTH.NY.GOV/facilities/long_term_care/retirement_communities/continuing_care/docs/2019-08-14_disclosure_statement.pdf)). **This is the key structural trick.** The life-care promise is backed by an existing SNF licence rather than new CON beds.

## 4. Why it works, and what a developer can and cannot replicate

### 4.1 The model's levers
| Lever | How RiverSpring uses it | Replicable by for-profit / JV? |
|---|---|---|
| **Land owned for decades** (32 ac, basis near zero, Hudson views) | Ground-leases about 3 ac to its affiliate CCRC for 99 years | **Partly.** Ground-lease from a nonprofit, church, hospital or legacy campus with underused acreage. RiverSpring itself bought its 14 ac from a religious order [V]. *Best sourcing channel.* |
| **Existing SNF licence** (843 beds) | Backs the life-care guarantee without new CON beds | **Hard.** New SNF beds are effectively unavailable in NY/NJ (CT exception for CCRC beds). Use a contract with an existing SNF operator, or buy beds/operator through a change-of-ownership filing (CHOW). |
| **Tax-exempt bonds** (Build NYC; 7.13% long yield) | 100% bond-financed start-up, about 86% presales | **Only via a 501(c)(3) sponsor/borrower.** For-profit: act as fee developer (ID2 played this role here [V]) or form a JV where the nonprofit owns the CCRC. |
| **Entrance fees** ($2.1M average) | First-generation entrance fees retire temporary bonds | **Yes**, with state CCRC certification. For-profit entrance-fee CCRCs exist (e.g., Edgehill Stamford, Benchmark-managed, taxable [V]). |
| **Medicaid managed care (MLTC)** | Scale and referral funnel ($1.27B revenue) | **No (practically).** NY MLTC licensing is closed and consolidating. RiverSpring itself exited [V]. |
| **Philanthropy** ($25M Starr gift, $100M endowment campaign, about $8M/yr) | Pre-development risk capital and liquidity support | **No** for a for-profit. A JV partner can bring it. |
| **Property-tax exemption / nonprofit status** | Lower operating cost | **No** for a for-profit. Some towns negotiate a PILOT. |
| **Mission / brand / NYP clinical affiliation** | Referral partner (79-bed Hauser rehab with NYP) | **Yes.** Hospital partnerships are open to anyone. |
| **Shelter / elder justice / research** | Reputation, grant revenue | Mission add-on, not economic. |

**Bottom line [I]:** Replicate the **real-estate layer** (IL/AL/MC on discounted, ground-leased or legacy land, near a hospital partner). Treat **SNF and health plan as partnerships**, not owned assets. For a life plan community, the only financeable structure is **nonprofit sponsor + for-profit fee developer/manager**, unless you go the taxable for-profit CCRC route (Edgehill model), which carries higher capital cost.

### 5.4 Practical implication matrix [I]
| Component | NY (≤ 2 hr) | NJ (≤ 2 hr) | CT (≤ 2.5 hr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rental IL | As-of-right where zoning allows (special permit typical) | Same | Same |
| Private-pay AL / MC | Yes: ALR/EALR/SNALR licence | Yes: expedited CN + Medicaid set-aside | **Yes: no CON** (easiest) |
| Medicaid AL | ALP allocation only | Set-aside units | Limited (CHCPE waiver) |
| SNF (new) | Effectively no; buy/contract | Effectively no; Add-a-Bed only | **Yes, if CCRC-associated and non-Medicaid** |

**Implication [I]:** CT is the most permissive state for a full life plan community with its own health center. Edgehill and Whitney are the models: own SNF beds inside the CCRC without fighting the moratorium. NJ and NY favor IL/AL/MC rental, plus a contract or JV with an existing SNF operator for the continuum.

## 6. Replicable program: prototypes and underwriting

> **All Section 6 figures are [I]: author estimates for 2026 NY-metro pricing.** Only the River's Edge cost anchors cited above are [V]. Benchmarks to confirm in the next pass: RLB/Cumming NY senior-living $/SF, NIC MAP margins, CBRE H1-2026 cap rates. The only verified cap-rate data in hand is CBRE 2023: Core Class C SNF **12.3%**, Class A active adult **5.2%**, all seniors housing +26 bps year over year ([CBRE 2023](https://www.cbre.com/insights/reports/us-seniors-housing-and-care-investor-survey)).

**Working benchmarks [I]:**
- **Stabilized NOI margins (after 5% management fee):** IL 38–42%, AL 28–33%, MC 25–30%, SNF 5–12%.
- **Cap rates (2025–26 Class A, Northeast):** IL 6.25–6.75%, AL 6.75–7.25%, MC 7.0–7.75%, entrance-fee CCRC 7–8%, SNF 11–13%.
- **Hard cost (NY metro suburban):** wood frame 3–4 stories $350–425/SF; podium / mid-rise concrete $450–550/SF; NYC high-rise $650–900/SF. Soft costs 20–25% of hard.

### Prototype A: Rental IL/AL/MC campus (core of the program)
- **Program:** 200 units = 100 IL / 70 AL / 30 MC. About 232,000 GSF (IL 1,400, AL 950, MC 850 GSF per unit including commons).
- **Site:** 10–15 acres. FAR about 0.35–0.45, in line with Osborn's 0.43 [V]. 3–4 stories. Parking about 1.0 per IL and 0.25 per AL/MC, plus staff, ≈ 140–160 spaces.
- **Target geography:** second-ring Westchester/Putnam/Rockland/Orange/Dutchess (NY); Morris/Somerset/Monmouth/Bergen fringe (NJ); New Haven/Fairfield fringe/Middlesex (CT).
- **Cost:** hard $375/SF → $87.0M; site work $5.0M; soft 20% → $17.4M; land $5.0M (12 ac × about $400k); financing / lease-up / FF&E $10.0M. **Total about $124M ≈ $620k per unit.**
- **Rents (exurban-affluent, 92% occupancy):** IL $6,000; AL $10,000 all-in; MC $12,500. Revenue: IL $6.62M + AL $7.73M + MC $4.14M + other 3% ($0.56M) ≈ **$19.0M**.
- **NOI:** 34% blended margin ≈ $6.5M, less $80k reserves → **$6.4M ($32k per unit) → YOC about 5.1%.**
- **To clear 6.5%:** cost at or below **$490k per unit**, or NOI at or above **$41k per unit** (needs AL $12k / MC $15k-type rents in Fairfield/Westchester, where land costs offset the gain).

YOC sensitivity (NOI per unit vs. all-in cost per unit):

| NOI/unit \ Cost/unit | $500k | $550k | $600k | $650k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $30k | 6.0% | 5.5% | 5.0% | 4.6% |
| $35k | **7.0%** | 6.4% | 5.8% | 5.4% |
| $40k | **8.0%** | **7.3%** | **6.7%** | 6.2% |
| $45k | **9.0%** | **8.2%** | **7.5%** | **6.9%** |

**Exit check:** $6.4M ÷ 6.75% ≈ $95M against a $124M cost. Value creation is **negative** at base case. Only the bottom-left cells (YOC ≥ 7.5%) create real spread. **Levers:** ground-lease from a nonprofit (removes land cost and lowers basis); AL/MC-heavy mix (higher revenue per SF); modular or wood frame; a hospital referral partnership to drive lease-up.

### Prototype B: Entrance-fee life plan community with health center (CT-preferred)
- **Program:** 250 IL (220 apartments + 30 villas) / 36 AL / 24 MC / 40 SNF (CCRC-associated, non-Medicaid, which fits the CT 17b-354 exception [V]). About 505,000 GSF.
- **Site:** 25–40 acres (comps: Edgehill 22, Seabury 68, Osborn 56).
- **Cost:** hard $425/SF → $215M; site $15M; soft 22% → $47M; land 30 ac × $250k = $7.5M; capitalized interest / DSRF / marketing about $55M. **Total ≈ $340M (about $970k per total unit).**
- **Fees:** average entrance fee about $900k (90% refundable, suburban CT/NY). River's Edge in NYC is $2.1M; suburban comps run about $0.5–1.2M [I]. Monthly fee about $6,000.
- **Capital stack:** initial entrance fees about **$225M** retire temporary debt. Permanent debt about $115M at about 6.5–7% (tax-exempt if a nonprofit sponsor; River's Edge long yield 7.13% [V]).
- **Stabilized operations:**
  - Monthly fees: $16.7M (250 × 93% × $6,000 × 12) + second-person fees about $1.7M + health center external revenue about $6M ≈ **$24–25M revenue**. Operating cash margin before entrance fees about 20–25% → **$5–6M**.
  - Net turnover entrance fees about 25 units/yr × about $200k net (new fee minus 90% refund) ≈ **$5M per year**.
  - Cash available for debt service about $10.5M against about $8.3M annual debt service → **DSCR about 1.3–1.4x** (River's Edge plan showed 1.54x [V]).
- **Developer economics:** YOC is not the right metric. Entrance-fee CCRCs are judged on DSCR, days cash and presales (River's Edge: 86% presold, 909 days cash planned [V]). For a for-profit, returns come from a **development fee of about 4–5% of cost ($13–17M)**, a management contract, and possibly a land sale or ground lease to the sponsor. ID2 played this fee-developer role at River's Edge [V]. Equity at risk is about 3–5% of cost in pre-development.
- **Timeline risk:** 10% deposits only after state approval (NY Certificate of Authority). River's Edge took about 8 years from Certificate of Authority filing to bond close [V].

### Prototype C: Urban / infill vertical
- **Program:** 160 units = 60 IL / 70 AL / 30 MC. 6–10 stories concrete. About 176,000 GSF. Parking 0.3/unit (structured). Site 1.5–2.5 acres (inner-ring Westchester, Hudson County NJ, Stamford/Norwalk).
- **Cost:** hard $650/SF → $114M; soft 25% → $28.6M; land $20M; financing $15M → **about $178M ≈ $1.1M per unit**.
- **Rents:** IL $8,500 / AL $13,500 / MC $16,500 → revenue about **$22.2M**. NOI at 33% ≈ $7.3M → **YOC about 4.1%**.
- **Verdict:** this prototype does not pencil at 6.5% without ground-leased or contributed land and tax relief, or an entrance-fee structure like River's Edge (about $2.43M per unit financed at a $2.1M average entrance fee [V]). Treat C as an opportunistic JV format only: hospital land, a nonprofit sponsor's land, or conversion of an existing building.

### Program-level recommendations [I]
1. **Lead with Prototype A** in second-ring NY/NJ/CT towns that pass a 2017-RiverSpring-style penetration test (75+ households ≥ $75–100k income; penetration ≤ 6–8%; home values ≥ $600k). **Underwrite to a 7.5%+ YOC** so that 6.5% is the downside case.
2. **Source land from nonprofits** (religious orders, legacy Jewish/Lutheran homes, hospitals) via ground lease. This is exactly how RiverSpring assembled its 14 ac and now monetizes it.
3. **Get the SNF continuum by contract**, through a priority-access agreement with a local 4–5-star nonprofit SNF, as River's Edge does with Hebrew Home. Do not own CON beds outside CT CCRCs.
4. **Pursue Prototype B in CT as a JV with a 501(c)(3) sponsor** (tax-exempt bonds via CHEFA [V, Seabury]). The developer takes fee, management and land economics.
5. **Diligence items before the first site:** current NIC MAP occupancy and pipeline by trade area; CBRE/JLL 2026 cap rates; RLB/Cumming NY cost index; NY Article 46 for-profit eligibility; NJ CCRC/CN rules for resident-only beds; status of CT SB 288; NY ALP allocation calendar; local zoning (special permit, senior-housing overlay, affordable set-aside such as Westchester's 10% recommendation [V]).
