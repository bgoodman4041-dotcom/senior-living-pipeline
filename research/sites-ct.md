# Connecticut Senior Living Site Sourcing (within ~2.5 hr of NYC)

Prepared 2026-10-19 for Ben (CEO). This is for a RiverSpring-style campus: rental IL/AL/MC on 8-15 ac with 150-220 units, or a CCRC/life-plan campus on 20-40+ ac with 250-400 units. The hurdle is a 6.5% stabilized yield on cost.
Companion CSV: `/mnt/project-files/senior-living/research/sites-ct.csv`

**How to read this.** "Verified" means a linked source says it. "Inferred" means my estimate, and it needs checking. Drive times are my estimates of off-peak minutes to Midtown. Lat/lon values are approximate.

**Research limits.**
- The session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Several leads could not be checked further (Unilever Trumbull address and acreage, Eastwood and Candlewood listing status, Briarwood status in 2026).
- LoopNet and Crexi blocked automated reading, so I found no active brokered land listings for senior use beyond the Orange CBRE flyer.
- I could not pull town median home values or counts of affluent residents 75+. Get those from ESRI/NIC MAP before underwriting.

**Regulatory frame.** CT keeps its moratorium on new nursing home beds, so a new SNF needs CON approval and is effectively closed. The realistic paths are:
- IL plus AL as a Managed Residential Community, with care provided by a licensed ALSA.
- A CCRC registered with DSS, which needs a continuing-care disclosure statement.
- SNF beds only by buying an existing licensed facility. CT-08 Greenwich Woods currently holds 217 beds that are slated for demolition.

## Tier 1: strongest candidates

### CT-02 Trumbull, 48 Monroe Turnpike (former Oxford Health HQ), 17.6 ac. ENTITLED.
- **Zoning:** The zone change and site plan are approved (4-1 vote) after a three-year process that included an appeal and wetlands approval. Approved program is 340 units: 193 IL/AL/MC in the converted office building plus 147 active-adult units. (Verified: [NEREJ](https://beta.nerej.com/340-unit-senior-housing-campus-approved-in-trumbull), [SHN 2023](https://seniorhousingnews.com/2023/04/19/former-united-healthcare-offices-slated-for-senior-living-redevelopment/))
- **Owner:** Senior Living Development LLC (Mark De Pecol). Bought in 2018 for about $3.3-3.4M. The owner said it would either sell or JV with an operator. (Verified: [Westfair](https://westfaironline.com/?p=140739), [Patch 2018](https://patch.com/connecticut/trumbull/senior-living-complex-expected-trumbull))
- **Why it fits:** It is the cleanest match to the 150-220-unit IL/AL/MC prototype, and the town has already decided the use question. Reusing an existing building cuts hard cost, which helps reach the 6.5% yield.
- **To verify:** Whether the approvals are still valid or have expired, whether any operator JV has closed, the asking price, and sewer capacity (Inferred to be public).
- **Drive:** about 75 min (Inferred).

### CT-01 Wilton, 329/331/345 Belden Hill Rd (former School Sisters of Notre Dame campus), 38.17 ac
- **Zoning:** R-2A. Hines is pursuing text amendment #26-426, which would add a CCRC definition, plus a special permit for a 280-unit CCRC: 36 IL cottages, 172 IL apartments, 48 AL and 24 MC. Hearings are expected from late September 2026. (Verified: [Wilton reg26 file](https://www.wiltonct.gov/planning-zoning-commission/files/reg26-428), [GMW Aug 2026](https://goodmorningwilton.com/ssnd-project-supporters-back-hines-with-conditions/))
- **Prior use:** Convent retirement housing plus a 40-bed SNF that closed in 2019. The site is wired to sewer through a 2013 agreement, but that agreement carries a deed restriction capping expansion at 10% of gross floor area, and the town must sign off to release it. (Verified: [GMW May 2025](https://goodmorningwilton.com/wilton-pz-commission-meeting-school-sisters-deed-restriction-may-12-2025/))
- **Risk:** HIGH.
  - The Wilton Neighbors Alliance opposes it, citing traffic, sewer, EMS load and precedent.
  - A neighbor has appealed the June 2026 wetlands permit.
  - The owner trust is appealing the town's tax-exemption decision.
  - On the other side, a "Support the Sisters" group is organized.
- **Play:** Hines holds the contract, so this is a backup-buyer or JV position, or a live test of whether Fairfield County will accept a CCRC. It matches the life-plan prototype almost exactly.
- **Drive:** about 70 min (Inferred).

### CT-03 Woodbridge, Country Club of Woodbridge (town-owned), ~152-155 ac, of which ~26 ac would be developed
- **Town plan:** The town's master plan (Selectmen consensus May 2025) lists a 100-unit assisted living building (~99k SF) at the former clubhouse, 32 senior cottages and 24-30 senior townhomes. It requires a new Zoning Overlay District, then a call for developer interest, then a Town Meeting vote. Sewer reaches the clubhouse; water comes through a private pump; both need replacement as a town capital cost. (Verified: [CCW Master Plan Final](https://www.woodbridgect.org/DocumentCenter/View/7154/CCW-Master-Plan-Final))
- **Risk:** MED-HIGH. The Board of Selectmen is split: an October 2025 motion on next steps failed on the First Selectman's tie-breaking vote. Residents are pushing back on density. (Verified: [Woodbridge Town News](https://www.woodbridgetownnews.com/news/letter-our-land-our-legacy-what-youre-not-being-told-about-the-ccw-master-plan))
- **Play:** Position early for the developer solicitation. A nonprofit, RiverSpring-style AL/MC plus senior cottages matches what the town itself drew. This would be a ground lease, so land cost is low.
- **Drive:** about 95 min (Inferred).

### CT-05 Simsbury, 140/200 Hopmeadow St (former The Hartford campus), 172-177 ac
- **Status:** A Silverman Group affiliate owns it. Its 518-unit plan was effectively denied in February 2024. A new 689-unit plan with no senior component was in wetlands review in May 2026. A 120-unit AL facility was already built on part of the campus. (Verified: [Commercial Record 2026](https://commercialrecord.com/2026/05/developer-pitching-almost-700-units-for-simsbury-ex-offices/), [Patch 2024](https://patch.com/connecticut/simsbury/massive-simsbury-development-project-all-dead), [HBJ](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/540-additional-residential-units-planned-for-the-hartfords-former-simsbury-campus/))
- **Town evidence:** In July 2026 Simsbury approved McLean's 40-unit IL expansion through a text amendment and special exception. Neighbors appealed that approval in court. (Verified: [Patch](https://patch.com/connecticut/simsbury/controversial-decision-senior-living-expansion-simsbury-being-challenged-court))
- **Play:** Offer Silverman a 25-40 ac CCRC carve-out. Senior units add fewer school children and less peak traffic, which answers the objections residents raised in 2024. This is my inference, but it is a strong argument.
- **Drive:** about 2 hr 5 min (Inferred).

## Tier 2: worth pursuing

- **CT-06 Southington, 2279 Mount Vernon Rd (former Briarwood/Lincoln College), 32.75 ac, existing campus buildings.**
  - PGX Holdings paid $3.5M. It withdrew a 55+ rezoning to the town's ARCHZ (Age-Restricted Cluster Housing) zone, about 165 rentals, on April 30 2025, after neighbor and commission pushback.
  - Earlier asking price was $8.92M through Colliers.
  - The owner may now sell. ([Patch](https://patch.com/connecticut/southington/plans-former-campus-redevelopment-project-pulled-southington), [HBJ](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/new-haven-area-company-buys-former-briarwood-college-campus/), [HBJ listing](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/slew-of-ct-college-campuses-for-sale-amid-post-pandemic-reset-for-higher-ed-industry/))
- **CT-04 Wallingford, 110 Leigus Rd (Campus at Greenhill), ~100 ac.**
  - KS Partners bought it for $9.1M and said it will talk with the town about developing portions of the land.
  - Zoning and utilities are unknown. ([HBJ](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/mass-real-estate-firm-aims-to-revive-struggling-wallingford-office-campus-after-9-13m-purchase/))
- **CT-07 Orange, 584 Derby-Milford Rd, 46.9 ac, 70,680 SF building.**
  - Zoned OPD (Office Park District); CBRE markets it for lease or redevelopment.
  - City water but septic, and senior housing is not a marketed use, so it would need a text amendment. ([CBRE flyer](https://images1.showcase.com/d2/OXpj48pQxUvpB0xpVmTGrQM0pBhCptmXUanpimPYTx4/document.pdf))
- **CT-10 Windsor, 1 Griffin Rd N, ~73 ac.**
  - Mark Greenberg bought it for $7.5M and is exploring apartments, industrial and self-storage on the excess land.
  - A senior parcel would have to be negotiated with him. ([HBJ](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/prominent-developer-greenberg-acquires-the-hartfords-windsor-office-campus/))
- **CT-08 Greenwich, 1165 King St (Greenwich Woods), 15.8 ac.**
  - Approved November 25 2025 for 170 units under 8-30g, the affordable-housing statute. These are not senior-restricted, and they replace a 217-bed SNF.
  - Zoning permits depend on a Westchester sewer agreement, and the Byram Neighborhood Association opposes any on-site sewage discharge.
  - It is interesting as a source of existing CT SNF beds or as a senior re-plan. Either would need a new approval. ([Patch](https://patch.com/connecticut/greenwich/plan-demolish-nursing-home-170-unit-assisted-housing-development-okd-greenwich), [Greenwich Free Press](https://greenwichfreepress.com/?p=238545))
- **CT-09 New Canaan, Waveny Park Parcel E, ~20 ac (town land).**
  - Waveny LifeCare Network proposed 150 units in July 2026 on a ground lease. Partner-only.
  - The article reports that 16% of residents are seniors and the town has only about 80 age-restricted units, which is strong demand evidence.
  - Opposition is high, including deed-restriction claims. ([New Canaanite](https://newcanaanite.com/?p=15113234))
- **CT-11 Trumbull, Unilever R&D campus.**
  - Unilever will leave Trumbull for a New Haven center opening 2029.
  - Address and acreage are not verified. Trumbull's approval of CT-02 is a favorable precedent. ([NBC CT](https://www.nbcconnecticut.com/news/local/unilever-to-move-trumbull-operations-to-new-haven-bring-300-jobs/3740082/))

**Patterns (Inferred).**
- Fairfield County towns approve senior housing when the developer reuses an existing building or footprint, as in Trumbull. They resist new density in single-family zones, as in Wilton, Newtown and Southington.
- Town-owned land (Woodbridge, New Canaan, Newtown) gives a political path to a nonprofit, RiverSpring-style operator, but the process is slow.
- Opponents in CT routinely appeal (Wilton, Simsbury), so budget 12-24 months for entitlement and litigation.
