## 3. Capital markets, margins and construction cost

- **Development cost (CBRE, 2Q26):** a national average of **$388,830 per revenue unit**, or $364/SF, up 23.6% since 2023. Hard cost is 72.5% ($262.75/SF), soft 16.2%, site 8.1% ($16,000 to $36,600/unit), FF&E $11,900/unit. **Average return on cost is 8.1%, down from 8.2%.** [V] https://www.cbre.com/insights/briefs/2026-senior-housing-development-costs
- **NY metro cost: $475,000 to $600,000/unit all-in**, and higher with structured parking or podium construction [I]. This applies NYC-area cost indices of roughly 1.25 to 1.4x the national average, plus land at $50,000 to $100,000+/unit in Westchester, Fairfield and Bergen. A Welltower working figure of "roughly $450,000 per unit in core markets" is cited by MMCG [V-2].
- **Cap rates, CBRE H1 2026:** Class A AL in core markets **6.5%** (down 28 bps in six months); IL, AL, MC, active adult and SNF all compressed 11 to 20 bps. [V] cbre.com investor survey. **CBRE H2 2025:** IL Class A core about 6.1%, non-core 6.8%; AL non-core 7.2%; free-standing MC 9.5 to 9.6%; SNF 12.0 to 12.3%. [V-2, CBRE via MMCG]
- **Rent required for new development:** CBRE says new development needs rents **15 to 20% above current market** in core markets. [V-2]
- **NOI margins:** REIT operating portfolios average above 25% (mid-2025), the highest since 2018 [V, NIC]. Welltower same-store is 27.7% (1Q26) [V-2]. Typical stabilized margins are IL 38 to 45%, AL 28 to 35%, MC 25 to 32%, and a CCRC/LPC with SNF 15 to 25% [I].
- **Spread:** a 6.5% YOC against Class A exit caps of 6.1 to 6.5% gives a thin development spread of 0 to 40 bps in core NY metro. **Develop only where you can hit 7%+ untrended, or reach 6.5% with a land basis below market** (a church, hospital or nonprofit partnership, or a RiverSpring-style affiliated campus).

## 4. YOC sanity check: what rent does 6.5% need?

**Formula.** Required monthly revenue per occupied unit = (Cost per unit × 6.5%) ÷ margin ÷ stabilized occupancy ÷ 12.

| Product | Cost/unit [I] | Margin [I] | Occupancy | Required blended rent incl. care fees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IL (rental) | $450,000 | 40% | 93% | **about $6,550/mo** |
| AL | $500,000 | 32% | 92% | **about $9,200/mo** |
| MC | $550,000 | 30% | 90% | **about $11,000/mo** |
| AL/MC blend (70/30) at $525,000 | | 31% | 91% | **about $10,100/mo** |

Compared with section 2, an AL/MC building clears 6.5% only where base AL rents of about $8,500 or more are already achieved, so that care fees lift the blend to about $10,000. That limits the field to the top-income towns. An IL-heavy or IL-led campus (RiverSpring-style IL, AL and MC on one site) clears more easily, because IL's higher margin carries the AL. These are illustrative; replace the assumptions with a pro forma.

## 5. Demand methodology (for the market study and for town boards)

**Step 1. Primary market area (PMA).** Use a 5 to 10 mile radius, or 15 to 25 minutes' drive, in suburban NY/NJ/CT, tightening to 3 to 5 miles in dense areas. The rule of thumb is that **about 70 to 80% of residents come from the PMA** [I]. Adult-child influence matters a lot: 20 to 30% of AL move-ins relocate to be near an adult child [I]. "80% capture" usually means this PMA-origin share. It does not mean a capture rate to aim for.

**Step 2. Age- and income-qualified households (AL).**

> Qualified HH = HH age 75+ in PMA with income ≥ (annual base rent ÷ 0.80 to 0.90)
> plus HH 75+ below that threshold whose income + annuitized home equity (e.g. home value × 0.90 net of sale costs ÷ 4 to 5 yrs of stay) clears it.

For a $9,000/mo AL rent, the income threshold is about $120,000 to $135,000 with income alone. Adding home equity drops it to about $60,000 to $80,000 in counties with median values of $550,000 to $700,000. **This is why the home-value column matters as much as the 65+ income column.** Use ACS B19037 (income by age of householder) and B25007/B25077 for owner-occupancy and value.

**Step 3. Need (AL/MC).**

> AL demand units = Qualified HH 75+ × % needing help with 2+ ADLs (≈ 8 to 12% at 75+, rising steeply at 85+) × (1 ÷ PMA share, e.g. ÷ 0.75) − existing + pipeline competitive units

**MC.** Dementia prevalence is about 11% at 65+ and about 33% at 85+ (Alzheimer's Association) [I]. Apply this to the income-qualified 75+ base, and assume 15 to 25% of those people are in a residential setting [I].

**Step 4. IL.**

> IL demand = Qualified HH 75+ (income ≥ annual rent ÷ 0.60 to 0.70, plus equity) × propensity (≈ 10 to 15%) ÷ PMA share − supply

**Step 5. Penetration and capture benchmarks** [I]. These are industry rules of thumb used by NIC, appraisers and lenders.

- **Market penetration rate** = (existing + planned competitive units) ÷ age/income-qualified 75+ households.
  - **IL:** under 8 to 10% is underserved; 10 to 15% is typical of mature markets.
  - **AL:** under 5 to 6% is underserved; 6 to 10% is typical.
  - Nationally, NIC primary markets have about **10 to 12 total senior housing units per 100 people aged 75+**.
- **Project capture rate** = project units ÷ (unmet qualified demand in the PMA).
  - Lenders want **under 10 to 15%** for AL/MC and under 15 to 20% for IL. Above 25% is a red flag.
- **Absorption:** 2 to 4 units/month for AL and 3 to 6 for IL. At current NIC absorption of 3.5 to 4.5%/yr [V], lease-up is faster in the supply-constrained Northeast.

**For town approval** (Ben's point that the town has to approve). NY, NJ and CT municipalities usually rule through a special permit or senior-housing overlay zone, and many ask for a **needs/market study plus a fiscal-impact study**. Senior housing generates no schoolchildren and pays taxes or PILOT, which is the strongest argument to a board. Pair the penetration and capture analysis with local data from section 6 and the county CSV: the count of 75+ residents, 65+ householder income and home values. For AL with a Medicaid or affordable component, NY ALP and NJ CN conditions add a state-level need test (section 7).

## 7. Regulatory overlay

**New York**
- **Nursing homes (RHCF):** full Certificate of Need under PHL Article 28, reviewed by DOH and approved by PHHPC, with a public-need methodology (10 NYCRR 709.3). New beds are rarely approved in the NYC region, so growth usually comes by **acquiring or decertifying existing beds**. [I; DOH CON page confirms nursing homes are under CON: https://www.health.ny.gov/facilities/cons/ [V]]
- **AL:** an Adult Care Facility license (adult home or enriched housing, SSL Article 7), with establishment approved by PHHPC on character, competence and financial feasibility. On top of that, **ALR / Enhanced ALR (aging in place) / Special Needs ALR (dementia)** certification under PHL Article 46-B. Private-pay ACF/ALR has **no bed-need CON test** but is a 9 to 18 month PHHPC process. **ALP** (Medicaid AL) beds are awarded by DOH solicitation against need. [I]
- **CCRC/LPC** (RiverSpring-style campus with an entrance fee): PHL Articles 46/46-A, approved by the CCRC Council (DOH/DFS). An SNF component brings in Article 28. [I]
- **IL** is unlicensed: zoning only.

**New Jersey**
- **CN:** long-term care (nursing home) beds need a **full-review Certificate of Need**. **New assisted living facilities go through expedited CN review** (N.J.A.C. 8:33, decided by the Deputy Commissioner). [V] https://www.nj.gov/health/healthfacilities/certificate-need/
- **AL conditions:** AL is licensed under N.J.A.C. 8:36. CN approvals for AL have historically carried a **Medicaid-occupancy commitment of about 10%** within three years of licensure [I; confirm against current rule].
- **CCRC:** registered with DCA under the Continuing Care Retirement Community Regulation and Financial Disclosure Act [I].

**Connecticut**
- **Nursing homes:** a long-standing **moratorium on new nursing home beds** (CGS §17b-354, administered by DSS), with narrow exceptions, so SNF growth means buying beds. Other CON is handled by the Office of Health Strategy. [I]
- **AL:** a **Managed Residential Community plus a DPH-licensed Assisted Living Services Agency** (ALSA). **No CON**, which is the main regulatory advantage of Fairfield County.
- **CCRC:** registration and disclosure with DSS [I].

**Implication.** The fastest paths are CT AL/MC (no CON) and NY/NJ IL plus private-pay AL/MC. Copying RiverSpring's skilled-nursing anchor means buying an existing licensed nursing home and repositioning or redeveloping the site. That is the classic RiverSpring/Hebrew Home strategy: build a campus around existing beds.

## 8. Top 12 submarkets (town clusters)

Ranking criteria, in order:
1. Rent support: 65+ income above $95,000 and home values above $600,000, so equity-qualified buyers can pay $9,000+ AL or $6,500+ IL.
2. A demonstrated $8,500 to $12,500 rent ceiling at existing communities.
3. Depth of the 75+ base within 10 miles.
4. A supply gap: aging pre-2000 stock and few or no new openings, in a metro already at 91% or more.
5. Drive time and entitlement path.
